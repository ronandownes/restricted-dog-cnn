The mechanism

How Grad-CAM produces a heatmap

Grad-CAM uses gradients for the selected class to weight deep feature maps. The combined activation map is then resized and overlaid on the original image.

What it can tell us Which image regions were most strongly associated with the prediction.
What it cannot tell us It does not prove causality or demonstrate that the model reasoned in the same way as a person.

Interpretation

Read patterns, not just pictures

Compare correct and incorrect cases from the same breed. Ask whether attention follows the dog, a body region, the background, image composition or a feature shared across breeds.

01

Is the model looking at the dog?

Check whether the strongest activation regions fall on the animal rather than the background.

02

Which body regions dominate?

Look for attention around the head, ears, muzzle, torso, coat pattern or overall silhouette.

03

Compare success and failure

Contrast true and false predictions from the same breed to identify unstable visual evidence.

04

Do not overclaim

A plausible-looking heatmap does not make the model legally reliable or suitable for breed identification.
